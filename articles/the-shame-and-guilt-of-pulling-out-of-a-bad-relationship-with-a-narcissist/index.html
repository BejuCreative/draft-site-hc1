The Shame and Guilt of Pulling Out of a Bad Relationship with a Narcissist!

By Dr. Judy RosenbergLicensed Clinical Psychologist June 4, 2024·5 min read

Leaving a narcissistic relationship can be one of the most liberating and empowering decisions you'll ever make, but it's not without its challenges. While you may have endured emotional abuse, manipulation, and toxic behaviour for an extended period, one of the most complex and persistent emotions that can haunt you when you finally decide to break free is guilt. In this blog, we will delve into the intricate web of emotions associated with leaving a narcissistic relationship and explore how to navigate the overwhelming guilt that often accompanies this journey.

Understanding the Guilt

You are not abandoning the narcissist, you are simply stopping the self abandonment.

Dr. Judy Rosenberg

Guilt is a complex and multifaceted emotion that can manifest in various ways when leaving a narcissistic relationship. To grasp its origins and effects, it is crucial to explore the following aspects:

  • Gaslighting and Manipulation: Narcissists are master manipulators and expert gas-lighters. They can convince you that you are the one at fault, that you are the problem in the relationship. None of us is perfect and often when with a narcissist we find ourselves acting and reacting in ways that are out of character for us. This can make you question your own judgment and contribution to the relationship's issues, leading to feelings of guilt.
  • Conditional Affection: In the early stages of the relationship, narcissists often employ "love-bombing," showering you with affection, attention, and compliments. You share special moments and memories together and they may have really put in effort to bring these about. As you contemplate leaving, you may feel guilty for "abandoning" someone who once seemed so loving and devoted.
  • Opinions of Others: Leaving a narcissistic relationship is never easy, and you may fear the judgment and criticism of others. You may worry that people will not understand your decision or even blame you for the relationship's problems. Many victims are made to feel as if they are the problem and the cause of the demise of the relationship by those who just do not understand and cannot see beyond the charm.
  • Feeling Responsible for Their Behaviour: Narcissists may frequently project their shortcomings onto their partners. They blame you for their actions or emotions, and you might internalize this blame, believing that you are indeed the cause of their problems.
  • Love and Empathy: Despite the toxicity, love, and empathy you feel for the narcissist can make you feel guilty about leaving. You may genuinely care for them and worry about their well-being.
  • Promises of Change: Narcissists are often skilled at promising change. They may pledge to work on the relationship and improve their behavior. When you decide to leave, the guilt may come from feeling like you're not giving them a chance to change, even though their history suggests otherwise.
  • The Good Times: It's common in narcissistic relationships to have periods of happiness and genuine connection amidst the turmoil. The guilt can arise from the idea of leaving the person who, at times, made you feel loved and valued.

Leaving a narcissistic relationship is a monumental step toward healing and personal growth. Here are some strategies to help you cope with the guilt that may arise during this journey:

  • Self-Compassion: Recognize that the guilt you feel is not your fault. Practice self-compassion and self-kindness. Remember that you deserve happiness and a healthy relationship.
  • Therapy and Support: Seek professional therapy or support from trusted friends and family. Talking to a therapist can help you navigate your emotions and gain a deeper understanding of the relationship dynamics.
  • Educate Yourself: Knowledge is power. Learn about narcissistic personality disorder and the patterns of behavior associated with it. This understanding can empower you and reinforce your decision to leave.
  • Establish Boundaries: As you prepare to leave, set clear boundaries to protect yourself from further manipulation. This might include limiting communication to essential matters and avoiding contact when possible.
  • No Contact Rule: Consider implementing a strict "no contact" rule. This can be one of the most effective ways to heal and regain your sense of self. It prevents the narcissist from exerting control over your emotions and decision-making.
  • Focus on Healing: Redirect your energy toward healing and self-care. Engage in activities that bring you joy and fulfilment. This not only aids in your recovery but also helps you regain your self-esteem and self-worth.
  • Support Network: Lean on your support network for encouragement and understanding. Share your feelings with friends and family who genuinely care about your well-being.

It's crucial to reframe your perspective when dealing with guilt after leaving a narcissistic relationship. Remember that you are not responsible for the narcissist's behaviour. Each individual is responsible for their actions, and you are not accountable for their choices. It is time to put your well-being first and to recognize that leaving was an act of self-preservation, not abandonment. You have the right to live a life free from emotional abuse and manipulation.

Likewise, we have to accept our limitations and understand that nobody is perfect, and no relationship is without its flaws. You are not to blame for the relationship's problems. Every relationship takes two willing participants.

On the same hand, you are not abandoning the narcissist, you are simply stopping the self abandonment. Narcissists always have replacement supply lined up with numerous people and so the image you may have of them pining away alone, for you, is just not accurate. It is time to deal with this situation as if closing up a failed business venture. You learn from the mistakes, get whatever is rightly yours from what is left and move on to better things.

Leaving a narcissistic relationship is a journey of courage and resilience. While guilt may be an unwelcome companion on this path, it should not deter you from breaking free and embarking on the path to self-discovery and healing. Recognize that your well-being is worth fighting for, and as you continue to heal and grow, the shadows of guilt will gradually dissipate. Embrace self-compassion, seek help, and focus on your journey of recovery. Reach out to us here at the Psychological Healing Centre for all the help you need. You deserve a life free from the chains of a narcissistic relationship, and we would love to help you do this.
